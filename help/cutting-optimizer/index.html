Rebar Cutting Optimizer: guide

How to get the fewest 12 m bars and the least waste for a list of cut lengths, and how to read the plan.

What the tool does

You list the cut lengths you need. The optimizer works out how to cut them from stock bars, by default 12 m, so that as few bars as possible are bought. It shows each cutting pattern, the waste, the reusable offcuts and the weight to buy per diameter, and tells you when the number of bars is proven to be the minimum.

When to use it

  • Before ordering, to know how many 12 m bars and tonnes each diameter needs.
  • To give the workshop a cutting plan with fewer offcuts.
  • After the BBS Analyzer, which sends its grouped cut lengths here.

Step by step

1. Set the stock bar and the saw

  • Stock bar length, m: 12 is the standard Saudi length. Change it only if you really buy another length (1–30 m).
  • Kerf, mm: the material one cut removes. 0 for shear cutting; about 3 mm for a saw. Whole millimetres, 0–20.
  • Allowance per piece, mm: optional extra length added to every piece (0–200). It is delivered with the piece and counted as loss.
  • Reusable offcut from, m: leftovers at least this long are listed as reusable; shorter ones are scrap. Use the shortest piece your site really reuses, for example 1 m.

2. Enter the cut list

One line per diameter, grade and cut length: Ø mm, grade (Gr60 or Gr80), length in metres and pieces. Pieces with different diameters or grades are never put on the same bar. Load example fills a sample list.

3. Or paste from Excel

Open Paste from Excel, paste columns in this order: diameter, length in metres, pieces, and optionally grade. A header row is skipped. Lengths above 100 are read as millimetres. Check the imported lines before you optimise.

4. Optimise

Press Optimise cutting. If a line is wrong, it is outlined and the message says why (for example a piece longer than the stock bar).

Worked example

16 mm Gr60 for footings

Settings: stock 12 m, kerf 0, allowance 0, reusable from 1 m. Cut list: 96 × 2.35 m, 64 × 3.60 m, 40 × 4.25 m.

32 ×4.25 + 3.60 + 3.600.55 m scrap
18 ×2.35 + 2.35 + 2.35 + 2.35 + 2.350.25 m scrap
4 ×4.25 + 4.25 + 2.351.15 m reusable

Result: 55 bars of 12 m, proven minimum. Required 626 m, bought 660 m, waste 34 m (5.15%), of which 11.90 m are reusable offcuts (4 × 1.15 m and 1 × 7.30 m) and 22.10 m scrap. Utilisation 94.85%. Weight to buy: 660 m × 1.578 kg/m = 1.042 t.

Reading the results

  • Status line. “Proven minimum number of bars” means the plan uses as many bars as a mathematical lower bound, so no plan can use fewer; with one stock length that is also the least waste. “Good plan, not proven minimum” means the plan is valid but the tool could not prove it is the best; the number shown is the most it could be above the minimum.
  • Stock bars, utilisation, waste, weight to buy. Totals for all diameters. Weight is nominal (0.006165 × d²).
  • Summary table. One line per diameter and grade, with its own result.
  • Cutting patterns. “32 ×” means 32 bars are cut the same way. Blue blocks are the pieces, a thin amber sliver is kerf, and the end block is the offcut: light amber if reusable, hatched if scrap.
  • Waste includes kerf, allowance and every offcut. Reusable offcuts are listed separately because you may use them elsewhere.

Common mistakes

  • Typing lengths in millimetres in the length column (2350 instead of 2.35). The column is in metres; pasted values above 100 are converted, typed values are not.
  • Using a saw kerf for shear-cut bars, or 0 for a saw: the plan will not match the workshop.
  • Setting “reusable from” too low: offcuts the site will throw away are counted as reusable.
  • Mixing grades under one grade name to “save” bars: grades are separate products and must stay separate.
  • Using bent-bar dimensions instead of the cut length: enter the cut length from the BBS.

Limitations

  • One stock length per run.
  • It does not calculate cut lengths from shape codes; use the BBS cut length or RebarPlan.
  • Weights are nominal, not weighed or invoiced.
  • A planning aid: check lengths against approved shop drawings before cutting. It is not a quotation.
