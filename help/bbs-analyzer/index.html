BBS Analyzer: guide

How to turn a bar bending schedule into checked quantities and theoretical weights, and send the cut lengths to the cutting optimizer.

What the tool does

The analyzer reads a bar bending schedule (BBS), recognises its columns, checks every row, and totals bars, length and theoretical weight by diameter, grade and shape. It never fills in missing engineering data: a row it cannot read is excluded and listed with the reason.

When to use it

  • To check a BBS from a consultant or subcontractor before ordering.
  • To get tonnes per diameter and grade for a quotation request.
  • To prepare the cut lengths for the cutting optimizer.

Step by step

1. Open the schedule

  • Excel .xlsx (also .xlsm; macros are never run). Choose the sheet if the workbook has several. Old .xls files must be saved as .xlsx or CSV first.
  • CSV or TSV. Comma, semicolon or tab separated.
  • Paste from Excel. Select the schedule including its header row, copy, paste.
  • Enter by hand. Member, bar mark, diameter, grade, shape code, cut length in mm and total bars.

Limits: 25 MB per file, 100,000 rows. Nothing is uploaded.

2. Check the columns

The analyzer looks for the header in the first 30 rows and matches columns by name in English, Arabic or Chinese (Bar mark, Dia, Cut length, No. of members, رقم السيخ, القطر, 钢筋编号, 下料长度…). Check every field and change any that is wrong.

  • Required: diameter, cut length, and the number of bars: either a total column, or “no. of members” and “no. in each” together.
  • Optional: bar mark, member, grade, shape code, and the sheet’s own total length and weight, which are used only to cross-check.
  • Cut length unit: read from the header, for example “Cut length (mm)”. If the header does not say, you must choose mm or m; the tool does not guess.
  • Header row: change it if the title rows confuse the detection.

4. Fix excluded rows

Excluded rows are listed with row number, bar mark, diameter, cut length, bars and the reason. Correct the spreadsheet and load it again. Title, note and subtotal lines are excluded as “not a bar line”, which is expected.

Why bars over 12 m are excluded

A cut length above 12,000 mm cannot be cut from one 12 m stock bar. The analyzer excludes it from every total and from the optimizer hand-off, and shows its bars, length and theoretical weight separately so nothing is hidden. 12,000 mm itself is included. The decision is made on the value exactly as written: 12,000.001 mm is excluded.

Why a missing grade must be chosen by you

Grade 60 and Grade 80 are different products. Rows without a grade stay in the analysis as “no grade” but are not sent to the optimizer. If you know the grade, enter it in the third group, read which rows it applies to, tick the confirmation and press Assign grade. It applies to all rows without a grade and is marked as assigned by you. The tool never assumes a grade.

5. Send to the Cutting Optimizer

Press Send to Cutting Optimizer. Valid rows are grouped by diameter, grade and cut length (rounded to the millimetre) and the optimizer opens in a new tab with them. Review the lines there and press Optimise; nothing runs by itself. Your schedule stays unchanged in the analyzer tab.

Reading the results

  • Three groups tell you exactly where every row went: included, excluded with a reason, or included but held back from the optimizer.
  • Warnings need review but do not change the totals: repeated bar marks (checked within each member; without a member column they cannot be told apart), several grades, grade spellings, non-standard diameters, formula values, and sheet weights or lengths that disagree.
  • Tables by diameter, by diameter and grade, by grade and by shape add up to the same totals.
  • Download CSV exports the used rows, the summary and the excluded rows; Print / PDF prints the report.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving the cut length unit to chance: always check the unit the analyzer shows.
  • Mapping “Total length” as cut length: cut length is the length of one bar.
  • Expecting bars over 12 m to be cut: they are excluded by design.
  • Assigning a grade “to make the rows go through” when the drawing does not say: ask the designer instead.
  • Saving the workbook without recalculating: formula cells use the value Excel saved, and those rows are marked.

Limitations

  • .xls (old format), .xlsb and .ods are not supported; the reader supports the tested .xlsx structures.
  • Cut lengths are not calculated from shape dimensions.
  • Weights are theoretical estimates, not certified quantities.
  • One grade at a time can be assigned to all rows without a grade; different grades per row must be corrected in the sheet.
