Structural steel is specified by section and invoiced by the kilo. Pick a family, pick a size, set a length: the weight per metre, per piece, and per order follows. Nothing is looked up from a stored weight, every figure is computed from the section's own dimensions. m = A × 7,850 kg/m³ Weight comes from the cross-sectional area of the nominal geometry, not from a stored figure: flanges and web plus the root fillets for a beam, legs less the overlap for an angle, the annulus for a pipe. Dimensions for beams, channels and angles follow EN 10365 and EN 10056. A UPN flange tapers, and the formula treats it as one flat average thickness, which puts the channel family about 2% above the published UPN masses; bar and flat sizes are common market sizes and are marked with an asterisk until they are checked against an official table. These are nominal weights. What a mill delivers falls inside the tolerance band of the governing standard, and where it falls inside that band is a commercial question. I wrote about that here.
