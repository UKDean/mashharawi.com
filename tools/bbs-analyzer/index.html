      How it reads a schedule.

      The file is read in your browser. Excel workbooks are opened by a small built-in reader: values only. Formulas are never run; where a cell holds a formula, the value Excel saved is used and the row is marked. Macros are never opened.

      Columns are recognised by name in English, Arabic and Chinese (Bar mark, Dia, Cut length, No. of members, رقم السيخ, القطر, 钢筋编号, 下料长度 and so on). You can change any of them. The length unit comes from the header, for example “Cut length (mm)”; if the header does not say, you choose it. The tool never guesses.

      Every row is checked. Rows with a missing or unreadable diameter, length or number of bars are excluded and listed with the reason, never filled in. A length written with a comma (2,875) is refused because it could mean 2.875 or 2875. Values that only make sense in the other unit are refused, not converted.

      Any cut length above 12,000 mm, one 12 m stock bar, is excluded from every total and listed with the reason “Cut length exceeds the 12 m stock bar limit”; 12,000 mm itself is included. Repeated bar marks (checked within each member; without a member column they cannot be told apart), grades written in different ways, non-standard diameters, and weights in the sheet that differ from the theoretical weight are reported as warnings for review. Nothing is deleted.

      Send to Cutting Optimizer groups the valid rows by diameter, grade and cut length and opens the optimizer in a new tab with them, for you to review. Diameters and grades are never merged. Rows with no grade stay in the analysis but are not sent until you assign a grade yourself; Grade 60 or 80 is never assumed. Your schedule stays here unchanged.

      Common questions

      How is the weight of a bar bending schedule calculated?

      For each row: 0.006165 × d² kg/m × cut length in metres × number of bars. A 16 mm bar is 1.578 kg/m, so 96 bars of 2.35 m weigh about 356 kg. The analyzer adds these up by diameter, grade and shape. The result is a theoretical weight, not a weighed quantity.

      Which file types can I use?

      Excel .xlsx (and .xlsm, without running macros), CSV or TSV, a range pasted from Excel, or lines typed by hand. Old .xls files need to be saved as .xlsx or CSV first.

      Is my schedule uploaded?

      No. The file is read in your browser and nothing is sent or stored. Close the page and it is gone. No account is needed.

      What if my columns have different names?

      Pick the right column for each field in the mapping. Only diameter, cut length and number of bars are required. If the length header has no unit, choose mm or m.

      Why was a row excluded?

      The excluded rows list gives the reason for each: a missing or unreadable diameter, length or number of bars, a value that does not fit the unit, a cut length above 12 m (one stock bar), or a total that contradicts members × bars in each. The tool does not fill in missing engineering data.

      Can I optimise cutting from the schedule?

      Yes. Send to Cutting Optimizer opens the optimizer with the cut lengths grouped by diameter and grade. Check the lines there, then run it. Rows over 12 m are excluded from the analysis and never sent, and rows without a grade are sent only after you assign one.
