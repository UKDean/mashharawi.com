Common questions

How is the weight of reinforcing steel calculated?

From the nominal diameter: mass per metre = 0.006165 × d² kg/m, with d in millimetres. The constant comes from a steel density of 7,850 kg/m³. A 16 mm bar is 0.006165 × 16² = 1.578 kg/m, so a 12 m bar weighs about 18.94 kg.

How many bars are in one tonne?

Divide 1,000 kg by the weight of one bar. For 16 mm at 12 m that is 1,000 ÷ 18.94, about 52.8 bars. Enter a tonnage in the calculator and it gives the number of bars directly.

Why is the invoiced weight different from the calculated weight?

The calculator gives the nominal weight. A mill delivers inside the tolerance band of the governing standard, so the weighed steel can sit a little above or below the nominal figure and still comply. Where it falls inside that band is a commercial question, which is why the tolerance belongs in the contract.

Which bar lengths and diameters can I use?

Any. The common Saudi market sizes, 10 to 32 mm, are one tap away, and 12 m is the usual stock length, but both can be changed, including diameters not on the list.

Can I keep a record of an order?

Yes. Add several lines to build a multi-size order with totals, then copy the table or use Print / PDF to save a dated sheet you can attach to a quotation.

Is anything I type sent anywhere?

No. The calculation runs in your browser, and nothing you enter leaves the page.
