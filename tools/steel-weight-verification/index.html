    How the check works.

    Nominal unit weight = 0.006165 × d² kg/m, with d the nominal diameter in mm. It is the same constant used by the cutting optimizer and the BBS Analyzer. It depends on the diameter only: Grade 60 and Grade 80 bars have the same nominal weight per metre.

    Theoretical weight = unit weight × sample length in metres × number of bars. Difference = measured − theoretical. Deviation % = (measured − theoretical) ÷ theoretical × 100: positive when the sample is heavier than nominal, negative when it is lighter.

    Everything is calculated from the values as entered and rounded only on screen. Lengths and weights must use a dot for decimals; a comma is refused because 2,5 and 2,500 are ambiguous. Any positive length is accepted in the unit you choose, which is never changed or guessed; the only length limit is one 12 m stock bar (12,000 mm). A difference over 25% shows an input-check note asking you to re-check the unit, bars and diameter; it is not an acceptance limit.

    No acceptance limit is built in. If you enter a range from your own specification or contract, each sample is shown as inside or outside that range, and that is all: it is a comparison with your numbers, not a compliance decision.

    Several samples can be compared in one session. Samples of the same nominal diameter are also pooled: (sum measured − sum theoretical) ÷ sum theoretical × 100, shown next to the lowest and highest individual sample deviation so no single result is hidden. Across different diameters only the total masses are added; no combined percentage is ever calculated.

    Common questions

    How do I check the weight of a rebar sample?

    Cut a bar to a measured length, weigh it, and enter the diameter, length, number of bars and measured weight. The tool calculates the nominal weight as 0.006165 × d² kg/m × length × bars and gives the difference in kg and the deviation in percent.

    What does a negative deviation mean?

    The sample weighs less than its nominal weight. A positive deviation means it weighs more. Deviation % = (measured − theoretical) ÷ theoretical × 100.

    Does the tool say whether the steel passes?

    No. It has no built-in acceptance limits and does not certify steel. You can enter the range from your specification or contract, and the tool shows only whether each deviation is inside that range.

    Is the nominal weight different for Grade 60 and Grade 80?

    No. Nominal weight per metre comes from the nominal diameter and the density of steel, so it is the same for both grades. The grade field is for your records.

    Can I compare several samples?

    Yes. Add as many samples as you need. The table shows each one, and samples of the same diameter are also combined, with the lowest and highest deviation shown beside the combined value.

    Is anything I enter saved or sent?

    No. The calculation runs in your browser, nothing is stored, and no account is needed. The WhatsApp button asks first, then copies the summary and opens the chat; you decide whether to send it.
